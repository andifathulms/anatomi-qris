## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product, decisively - with two imported patches.**

**LLM assessment.** The visual language is derived from the artefact rather than applied to it, and the derivation is checkable in source. Colour is generated by the subject: ten role hues split warm-structure / cool-content (`tokens.css:41-50`), governed by a rule no other product could adopt without it meaning something different - chrome may never be `#000000` or `#ffffff` because the matrix owns those. The tint model is physical, not decorative: the hue rides the module value at 32%/65% coverage with a stated failure test. `--marker` magenta is argued for (the one hue the ten roles leave free, legible over both black and white paper) and then used with real discipline as the only selection signal across five unrelated views - module stroke, TLV row edge, bitstream outline, toggle underline, rail station. The 8px spacing base is the module grid, so a byte highlighted in three places is the same width in all three. The bitstream *refuses* hue and separates its five spans by value alone to protect the role budget; refusals like that are the strongest evidence of authorship.

Genuinely generic: the topbar is stock; the sample chooser is a bare `<select>` in a product where every other choice is a considered custom control; and the new colophon is the least specific block on the page - four social glyphs including Instagram, lifted from portfolio convention. It is also the only user-facing copy in the app that bypasses `id.ts`/`en.ts`.

One new contradiction: `--brand-magenta: #f0328c` sits three hex points from `--marker: #f0439a`. DESIGN.md §2.4 reserves the marker for selection and focus and nothing else; the brand mark now parks a visually identical magenta permanently in the top-left corner, on an element that is never selectable. The code comment resolves the rule conflict, not the perceptual one.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json src` -> exit 2, **1 finding total**: `layout-transition` at `src/views/DamageLab/damage-lab.css:49` (`transition: width` on the 10px capacity meter). Per-area: App.tsx 0, stages 0, ui 0, views 1. Genuine but negligible - one 10px element, no siblings, no text reflow. Accept it. Zero findings in the categories this project deliberately violates (pure black/white, no icon library, self-hosted fonts).

Also clean, mechanically: **zero** hardcoded colours in any CSS outside `tokens.css`; **zero** `outline:none` anywhere; no `<img>` without alt because there are none; every decorative `<svg>` correctly `aria-hidden`; `tsc --noEmit` clean; bundle 191.4/250 kB with the no-network assertion passing.

**Browser evidence.** Rendered at 1440, 1366, 390 and 380 px, both themes: **zero horizontal scroll at every width including 380**, zero console errors, and **zero off-origin requests** - the offline claim verified in a real browser rather than asserted. Three measured defects the source review could only suspect:

- Switching to Anatomy grows the specimen card **700 -> 939px** (+239) as the legend mounts inside it. Same rule.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-made instrument whose engineering discipline reaches further into the interface than most design systems ever manage: one provenance map feeds five views and nothing is allowed to guess. The craft floor is high and the token system is close to exemplary.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. It is that **the app opens on its weakest possible frame and buries its best one**. Stage 1 renders a QR code with its middle missing, under a title promising anatomy, with the largest type on the page (44px, right column) belonging to the stage panel rather than the specimen. The thing PRODUCT.md calls "the product" - the merchant's name lighting up as specific black squares - is three cards down on desktop, four on a phone, and reachable only by discovering that a column of numbers is draggable.

## What's Working

1. **One provenance map, five views, zero drift - and it is visible in the interface, not just the architecture.** `byCodeword`/`byStreamIndex` serve the tree highlight, damage attribution, codeword table and zigzag order. The highlighting *feels* trustworthy because no view is permitted to recompute geometry.
2. **The division trace renders the encoder's own record and says so.** `Division.tsx:30-35` reads `build.divisionTrace` directly; changed terms are marked by weight and underline rather than colour; the alpha/hex toggle makes multiplication and XOR legible in turn. It is the one Reed-Solomon view a person could actually check by hand.
3. **Colour is budgeted, and the budget is enforced by refusal.** The bitstream forgoes hue entirely; mask penalties reuse `--damage` instead of inventing a warning colour; the winning mask card gets a filled square and nothing else. That restraint is why the anatomy view stays readable at all.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The flagship interaction has a 16 x 20px hit target inside a 626 x 31px row that pretends to be clickable.**
- *Why it matters:* `payload-tree.css:55-57` lights the entire row on hover, but only the two-character tag is a button (`PayloadTree.tsx:86-89`). Users will click the merchant name and nothing will happen. This is the one interaction PRODUCT.md calls the product, and the target is below WCAG 2.5.8's 24 x 24 minimum.
- *Fix:* make the whole row the control - a button filling the row, or a click handler on the row with `tabIndex`/`role="button"` - and drop the tag-only button. The hover styling already implies it.
- *Command:* `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] The promised arrow-key matrix navigation is kept for sighted keyboard users and broken for screen readers, and cannot damage anything.**
- *Why it matters:* `Matrix.tsx:170-184` is `<svg role="img" tabIndex={0} onKeyDown>`. `role="img"` prunes descendants and is not a widget role, so in browse mode the reader consumes the arrows and `handleKey` never fires. Focus does not set `focusedModule`, so the readout says nothing. Separately, `handleKey` has no Enter/Space case, so the damage lab - the app's emotional payoff - is pointer-only, while `damage.hint` instructs the user to "drag across the matrix".
- *Fix:* `role="application"` or `role="grid"` with roving focus; set `focusedModule` on focus; wire `aria-describedby` to the readout instead of re-announcing six term/definition pairs per keypress; add Enter/Space to break the focused module.
- *Command:* `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] The topbar's trust claim reads as an error.**
- *Why it matters:* `'app.offline': 'Tidak ada koneksi'` (`id.ts:10`) is the standard Indonesian phrasing for "you have no internet", rendered beside a green dot. At the one moment the interface must say "your merchant data is safe here", it says something is wrong with your connection. PRD §9.1 makes this line load-bearing.
- *Fix:* make the chip a claim about the app, not the network - `Diproses di perangkat ini` - and keep the fuller guarantee in the console.
- *Command:* `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] On a phone, 37% of the viewport is chrome and the specimen is cropped.**
- *Why it matters:* measured at 390 x 844: topbar 134px + console 175px = 309px of fixed chrome, and the matrix is cut off mid-code by the sticky console on first paint. The rail loses its labels below 1180px, so on a 1366px laptop and every phone the nine stations are unlabelled numbered dots. PRD acceptance criterion 10 says the matrix stays the hero on a phone; right now you cannot see all of it.
- *Fix:* collapse the tagline out of the topbar under 720px (it already wraps to a third line at 380px), and let the console shrink to the field alone until focused. Give the rail short labels rather than none.
- *Command:* `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] `Perbaiki` silently no-ops on slider-induced damage.**
- *Why it matters:* `repair` filters `state.damage` (`store.tsx:121-124`), but noise damage lives in `state.noise` and is regenerated on read (`store.tsx:290-299`). The noise slider is the most likely route to the payoff, and the recovery button on that route appears dead. The damage brush also always paints 3 x 3 (`App.tsx:52-69`) despite its own comment promising 1 x 1 with Shift to widen - so a reader cannot demonstrate "one broken module corrupts exactly one codeword", the cleanest entry into the whole error-correction argument.
- *Fix:* have `repair` zero the noise and materialise surviving noise modules into `state.damage`; implement the documented 1 x 1 default with Shift for 3 x 3 and say so in `damage.hint`.
- *Command:* `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)** - the curious Indonesian PRD §4 says must get value in ten seconds without reading:
- The opening frame is a QR code with its middle missing (stage 1 paints every data/ECC module white). He recognises the object and it is broken, with no explanation. The complete code is at stage 8.
- Nothing indicates the rail is the control or that it is draggable. No play button, no "start here", no motion on load.
- Below 1180px the rail loses its names entirely - his 1366px laptop shows nine numbered dots.
- The one thing that would hook him - "Warung Sae, Sleman, warung, ID" - is the third card down on desktop, fourth on a phone, behind a stat grid reading "Versi 10 / byte / M / Mask 2".
- Stage 1's panel is the most technical in the app: data codewords, ECC codewords, blocks, repair capacity. That is the first prose he meets.

**Sam (Keyboard + Screen Reader)** - is DESIGN.md §8's promise kept? **Partially, and the headline half is not:**
- Arrow-key module navigation is implemented and works well *for a sighted keyboard user* - but `role="img"` puts it out of reach of a screen reader, and focusing the matrix sets no initial module.
- The damage lab is pointer-only. Sam reaches the payoff only through the coarse noise slider, never the brush.
- The stage rail is an invalid composite: `role="slider"` containing nine focusable buttons - ten tab stops for one control.
- The interleave grid's ARIA table is structurally broken: `role="cell"` spans are not children of their `role="row"` (`Interleave.tsx:64-97`).
- The console sits outside `<main>` with no landmark, so landmark navigation skips the payload field entirely; in tab order it comes after the rail, the matrix and a stage panel that at stage 2 is one button per codeword (300+ at version 15).
- **Kept, and credited:** focus is a 2px marker outline at 2px offset, never removed, zero `outline:none` in the codebase; the skip link works; reduced motion is honoured properly including a step control replacing Play; nothing below 11px; and `CodewordTable` is a real text equivalent for the matrix, not a token gesture.

**Alex (Power User)** - the developer verifying Reed-Solomon by hand:
- Well served on the core path: generator polynomial, per-step lead term, remainder before/after, alpha/hex toggle, per-block selection, single-step advance.
- **No way to get the data out.** No copy affordance for the bitstream, codewords, trace or coordinate table; the only clipboard action copies the URL. For an app whose principle is "the arithmetic stays checkable", he ends up retyping hex or opening devtools.
- The state he sets up is destroyed by the controls he needs: changing EC clears forced mask, damage and highlight; forcing a mask clears damage. "Mask 3 vs mask 5 under identical damage" is impossible.
- Nothing is linkable at depth - stage, mask, TLV node and damage are all absent from the URL.

## Minor Observations

- **Measured layout shift, against DESIGN.md §8's explicit prohibition:** hovering a module grows the readout 72 -> 110px and the specimen card 700 -> 737px; switching to Anatomy grows the card 700 -> 939px as the legend mounts inside it. Reserve the readout's tallest state and the legend's height.
- `--brand-magenta` (#f0328c) vs `--marker` (#f0439a): two near-identical magentas with different meanings, one permanent and one meaning "selected".
- `.tlv__mark` is white on `--marker` at 3.51:1 in dark theme, under the app's own 4.5:1 floor; light theme is fine at 5.77:1.
- The specimen shows five chips; DESIGN.md §4.3 specifies four.
- `MakerSignature` hardcodes four English labels outside the copy system - the only strings in the app that do.
- The theme control offers dark/light but never "system", though `initialTheme()` follows the system preference. A one-way door.
- `Cite` opens a popover without moving focus into it and does not restore focus on close.
- Sample `note` fields are written, committed, and never rendered - exactly the one-line context Jordan needs.
- Detector's single finding (`transition: width` on the capacity meter) is real but negligible; accept it.
- `min-height: 100vh` on the shell rather than `100dvh` (`app.css:7`) - overshoots on mobile Safari with a dynamic toolbar.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Why does the app open on stage 1?** The visitor already knows what a finished QR code looks like; the mystery is what is inside it. What if the default were stage 8 - complete code, chips filled, tree populated, merchant name visible - and the invitation were to scrub *backwards* into the construction? Stage 1 becomes a revelation rather than an unexplained blank.
2. **If the connection between the two layers is the product, why is the payload tree an appendix?** Should it be a permanent peer of the specimen when the lens is active, rather than the third card down?
3. **Is the rail a spine, or a nine-item menu in a slider costume?** Nine equal-weight stations tell the reader that "Codeword" and "Kerusakan" matter equally. They do not.
4. **The payoff stage is the only one that cannot be operated from a keyboard.** If Enter broke the focused module, arrow-key navigation would stop being an accessibility checkbox and become the *precise* instrument the 3 x 3 pointer brush can never be - for everyone. What is lost by making the keyboard the better tool here?
5. **You built a complete provenance map and then refused to let anyone take any of it away.** No copy, no export, no deep link past `?t=`. Given that the offline guarantee makes clipboard and download strictly local, what is the argument against "copy these codewords" - and is it really the same argument as PRD §9.4's refusal to be a QR generator?
